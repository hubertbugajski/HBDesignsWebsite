Root cause

Field-failure root cause analysis

Systems that fail intermittently, or behave differently in the field than on the bench, usually involve more than one discipline at once. I make the problem reproducible, add diagnostics and telemetry where none exist, and separate firmware causes from hardware, mechanical, and environmental ones.

The goal is the actual mechanism — from logs, lab measurements, and real hardware behaviour.

Selected work

Inverter–generator synchronization

Schneider Electric XW Pro · via HB Designs

A generator-backed hybrid inverter system had a synchronization and control interaction that could go unstable under certain field conditions. I made it repeatable with HIL simulation and physical 20 kW generator testing, then worked with the controls team to identify the mechanism and validate the re-tuned controller behaviour. The fix shipped in a certified firmware release.

Generator switchover interruption>100 ms → <40 ms

68K platform brought back into production

OPI Systems

Flash memory obsolescence forced a firmware change on a legacy 68K product. The development environment was decades old and unsupported, the compiler vendor was gone, and the bootloader source was missing. I rebuilt the toolchain, recovered the bootloader source and put it under version control, and automated manufacturing programming by combining bootloader and application into a single JTAG-programmable artifact.

Production programming time10–15 min → <1 min

Robot localization and motion throughput

ATTAbotics

Worked on optical localization correction using camera and fiducial tracking, sensor-based correction on legacy robots, Time-of-Flight sensor fault detection, and motion-profile optimization. Extending the useful life of the legacy fleet helped defer roughly $750K in capital expenditure.

Presentation throughput, R&D testing<3 → 5.5 / min

Wireless monitoring device field failures

Wireless monitoring product

Devices were dropping sessions unexpectedly in the field. Working with ST microcontroller firmware and Wi-Fi/Bluetooth module integration — including a Redpine to Silicon Labs SDK migration — I added diagnostic telemetry and flags to capture device state ahead of the failures.

Root causeEnclosure water intrusion, not firmware

Wireless heated-clothing controller

Acquired by Gerbing Heated Clothing

A personal project that turned into a product: bare-metal PIC firmware, custom PCB, and a custom RF protocol on MAX7032 transceivers. The useful lesson was that a radio link needs real framing, timing, and protocol behaviour — not UART bytes pushed at an antenna. Gerbing reviewed the prototype and acquired the completed design and IP for production.

ScopeFirmware, hardware, and RF protocol

About

HB Designs is essentially me. I founded it in 2022 and I do the work myself — I’m not staffing this out or handing it to a junior.

Twenty-plus years in embedded systems: microcontrollers, RTOS, bare-metal firmware, and embedded Linux. Along the way that has meant certified inverter firmware, real-time robot control, wireless monitoring devices, applications engineering at Maxim Integrated, and a 68K platform that needed to keep shipping after its original tools disappeared.

I like the work that sits between disciplines — where a bug isn’t really a firmware bug or a hardware bug, it’s both, plus something environmental nobody accounted for. Writing correct C is table stakes. The harder and more useful skill is working out why real hardware, in the field, is doing something it isn’t supposed to do.

I work best on real products with real constraints: production schedules, hardware you can’t redesign, field data that doesn’t fully add up, and someone who needs an answer rather than a theory. I’m hands-on and technical, not a management consultant.

B.Eng. Electrical Engineering, Lakehead University · Electronics Engineering Technology diploma, Fanshawe College
