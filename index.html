Selected work

Hybrid inverter / generator oscillation

Schneider Electric XW Pro · via HB Designs

A generator-backed hybrid inverter system had a field issue where the inverter and generator could get into an unstable interaction under certain operating conditions. The behavior was not isolated to one firmware function. It involved real-time firmware behavior, inverter control loops, power electronics, and the generator's own voltage regulation.

The first step was to make the problem repeatable. Using Hardware-in-the-Loop simulation, I found the cause of the behavior in a controlled environment and then validated it against a physical 20 kW generator. Once the issue was repeatable, I worked with the controls team to understand the mechanism and validate the revised controller behavior.

The final result was a certified firmware fix for a real field issue on Schneider Electric's XW Pro hybrid inverter platform. This is the kind of embedded problem that does not fit neatly into "firmware" or "hardware." The useful work was making the failure reproducible, understanding the system interaction, and proving the fix under realistic conditions.

Generator transfer timing improvement

Schneider Electric XW Pro · via HB Designs

A hybrid inverter system needed better transfer behavior during generator/inverter switching. The existing interruption was over 100 ms, which was long enough to affect downstream equipment in some installations.

I worked on the firmware behavior around transfer timing and validation, including the interaction between firmware state machines, relay timing, synchronization behavior, and system-level power continuity. Small timing changes in embedded power systems can have large user-visible effects. This work required understanding firmware timing, power-system behavior, and how the product behaved outside ideal lab conditions.

Transfer interruption>100 ms → <40 ms

Legacy 68K platform recovery

OPI Systems

A legacy embedded product based on a Motorola 68K-derived platform lost production capability after a flash-memory component became obsolete. The product was decades old, the original toolchain was unsupported, the compiler vendor was gone, and the bootloader source code had been lost.

I restored the development environment, recovered the bootloader source, brought it into version control, updated the firmware path, and automated the manufacturing programming process. The new flow combined the bootloader and application into a JTAG-programmable artifact. Not every embedded problem is greenfield development. Sometimes the product still needs to ship, the tools are gone, the parts changed, and nobody wants to touch the code.

Production programming time10–15 min → <1 min

Wireless monitoring field failures

Wireless monitoring product

A wireless monitoring product using an ST microcontroller and Wi-Fi/Bluetooth vendor module was experiencing field reliability issues, including sessions ending unexpectedly. At first, the issue looked like it could be firmware, wireless connectivity, or a vendor SDK problem.

I worked through firmware integration during a Redpine/Silicon Labs SDK transition and added diagnostic flags and telemetry so the device could report more useful state before failure. That changed the debugging process from guessing to evidence. The diagnostic data helped point the issue away from firmware and toward enclosure water intrusion in the field.

For connected devices, "it disconnected" is not enough information. Firmware needs enough observability to tell the difference between a software bug, a wireless problem, a power issue, a mechanical issue, or the environment destroying the product.

Robotics localization and motion debugging

ATTAbotics

At ATTAbotics, robotic systems needed better localization correction, better fault detection, and higher motion throughput. The problems crossed firmware, controls, sensors, mechanics, and robot infrastructure.

I worked on optical localization correction using camera/fiducial tracking, sensor-based correction for legacy robots, Time-of-Flight sensor fault detection, and motion-profile optimization. Real robotic failures are often cross-domain. The fix may be in firmware, but the cause may involve mechanics, sensors, motor behavior, timing, or incorrect assumptions about the physical system.

The work improved robot behavior, extended the useful life of legacy robots, and helped defer approximately $750,000 in capital spending.

Presentation throughput, R&D testing<3 → 5.5 / min

Wireless heated-clothing controller

Acquired by Gerbing Heated Clothing

I designed a wireless heated-clothing controller originally for personal use. The system used bare-metal PIC firmware, a custom RF protocol, MAX7032 RF transceivers, and a production-ready PCB implementation. The first attempt was simple: send UART-like data into the radio. That quickly exposed why RF systems need proper framing, timing, preamble behavior, and protocol design.

I implemented the protocol in firmware and validated it against real RF hardware. Gerbing Heated Clothing later reviewed the prototype and acquired the completed design and associated IP for production deployment. This was a complete small embedded product: hardware, firmware, RF communication, PCB implementation, timing constraints, and product handoff.

About
